About

I am a Senior Associate Consultant at Infosys BPM in Bangalore. My work starts with the process: I run end-to-end assessments across BFSI domains to spot where IPA, RPA, GenAI and agentic AI can make a real difference.

From there I design the solution, build the business case, prototype it with the people who will use it, and work with architects and delivery teams to get it live. Along the way I manage and mentor teams, keep SLAs and KPIs on track, and write the proposals that get leadership to say yes.

AI product idea

Reforge

My own product: upload an SOP and get back a process map, an assessment, a suggested transformation route and a business case. Built as a team of six AI agents, each with its own skill file and strict handoff contracts between them. It reads scanned SOPs with OCR and draws the map as BPMN.

FastAPI, JavaScript, bpmn-js, Tesseract OCR, Gemini free tier. Live demo (passcode-gated, ask me).

AI experiment

Compliance Lab

A working prototype for compliance testing in US mortgage servicing: six AI workers with validated handoffs run hundreds of checks across a book of loans, flag the ones likely to go delinquent, and export an audit trail. Runs on synthetic data only. A lab, not a product, and I say that plainly when I demo it.

Python, offline OCR, 50+ automated tests. Private repo.

How I think

The Process Lab

An anonymised example, details changed, of how I turn a messy process into a decision. This is the shape of the work, not a real client.

As-is

  • New loan files land in a shared queue. Nobody owns the queue.
  • An analyst opens each file, reads the same six documents, and keys the same fields into two systems.
  • One in five files bounces back for missing documents, found days later.
  • Cycle time: measured in days. Nobody can say exactly how many.
→

To-be

  • Intake checks completeness on arrival and bounces incomplete files the same hour.
  • Documents are read once, fields extracted, and both systems updated together.
  • The analyst handles only the exceptions, with the extracted evidence attached.
  • Cycle time and first-pass rate are on a dashboard the team actually opens.

The technology choice comes last. First the process has to deserve it.

Rebuilding this site

Read an article about "lived-in websites" and recognised my own site as the showroom it warns about. So the brochure is coming down and this is going up: the same facts, but with the workshop door left open.

10 Oct 2026

Agents need contracts, not vibes

Reforge v2 went from one big prompt to six agents with skill files and handoff schemas. The lesson: without a strict handoff contract, agents drop steps quietly and smile about it. OCR on a scanned SOP came back at 96% confidence, which felt like magic until a rule split across two lines got missed. Tests caught it. Tests always catch it.

8 Oct 2026

Say what is simulated

Demoing the Compliance Lab taught me that a prototype's honesty is its best feature. Synthetic data, simulated orchestration, said out loud, and the room trusts the rest of the demo more, not less.

Sep 2026

Cheap is expensive

The first Reforge demo looked fine to me and "level one" to the person whose opinion I trust most. Rebuilt the UI from scratch. Took twice as long and was worth it. Taste is a requirement, not a garnish.

The approach

  1. Find the opportunity

    Walk the process end to end and turn operational pain points into clear automation and AI candidates.

  2. Make the case

    Design the AS-IS and TO-BE, then tie scope to revenue, cost, productivity and operational KPIs.

  3. Prototype early

    Put a working concept in front of business users so they can check the workflow before build starts.

  4. Take it to production

    Bridge business requirements with architects and delivery teams, with control and governance designed in from day one.
